# Career partners, learner recruitment, and use cases

Research date: 8 November 2026. Geographic starting point: Uyo/Akwa Ibom and Nigeria, based on Areté's current community and repository context; international training partnerships are a later option. Partner fit, priority, proposed asks, and acquisition targets below are our judgments. Public sources establish relevant activities and contact routes, not acceptance, funding, recruitment access, or vacancies.

The product context is the [career pathway implementation plan](career-pathway-implementation-plan.md). An editable shortlist is available in [the prospect CSV](career-partner-prospects.csv). The [pilot proposal](career-pilot-proposal.md) supplies a concrete initial offer, and [target users and use cases](career-users-and-use-cases.md) gives the product/recruitment list. All prospects start at `researched`; no email, meeting booking, partner application, or learner recruitment message has been sent.

## 1. What Areté should seek first

Prioritize small deliverable commitments:

1. **Learner access:** one community or university invites an opt-in pilot cohort.
2. **Assessment credibility:** practitioners review the pathway/rubric and provide a bounded number of marking sessions.
3. **Career relevance:** employers validate target tasks and review sample portfolios.
4. **Actual opportunities:** supervised projects, internships, apprenticeships, or interviews where the organization confirms a real opening and eligibility.
5. **Affordable delivery:** sponsors fund named cohort places, reviewer time, devices/data, or exam costs when terms are confirmed.
6. **Formal training relationships:** pursue academy/official training status after checking organizational, instructor, financial, and licensing requirements.

A partner can contribute one function without supplying all six. A training provider or community is not automatically a hiring partner; an employer conversation is not a promise of internships.

## 3. How to turn prospects into working partnerships

### Prepare a reviewable pilot pack

Before outreach, prepare a one-page offer and a short demonstration containing:

- Audience and problem: beginners need an explained career direction, assessed work, and support taking the next step toward work.
- Product status: what is usable now, a proposed quiz/result example, one practical task, one rubric, and one anonymized or explicitly fictional sample portfolio. Label prototypes clearly.
- Exact ask: one reviewer, one session, a capped cohort, a role-validation interview, or a specified number of sponsored places. Do not lead with an indefinite “partner with us.”
- What Areté provides, who delivers it, cohort size and dates, review turnaround and capacity, support and data-handling boundaries.
- An itemized budget: content/review/career-support hours × agreed rates, infrastructure, access support and contingency. Add real figures after obtaining rates; do not invent a sponsor price or partner fee.
- Three success measures: assessed work completed, useful career actions delivered, and partner effort/value. Include current user figures only when supported by current account/product data.

### Discovery questions

Ask every prospective partner: Which learner/role problem matters to you? Which contribution is feasible? Who can approve it? What does success look like? What are the time/budget requirements? What evidence and data can be shared? What would prevent a pilot?

For employers, additionally ask: What tasks can an entry-level person actually perform under supervision? Which missing skills prevent interviews? What portfolio evidence would you trust? Are there **current** opportunities, how many, with what requirements, pay arrangements, deadlines and supervision? Can you commit to reviewing a fixed number of relevant portfolios even if you cannot hire?

For training programs, ask about prerequisites, instructor approval, courseware rights, eligibility by country/entity, minimum commitments, fees, exam versus training costs, branding, result sharing, and renewal obligations. Verify legal or contractual requirements from the actual terms before committing.

### Operating sequence

| When | Activity | Concrete output |
|---|---|---|
| Days 1–5 | Product lead prepares pack; confirms first six routes and warm introductions | One-pager, demo, budget assumptions, prospect ownership and next action |
| Days 6–10 | Send a small number of tailored approaches after the founder approves sending | Logged initial outreach; target three discovery conversations, not mass messages |
| Days 11–20 | Run discovery and record exact needs/constraints | Proposed cohort/reviewer/employer role-validation scopes |
| Days 21–30 | Agree one small collaboration and staff it | Written responsibilities, dates, limits, cost and evaluation; first scheduled activity |
| After delivery | Review work, learner/partner feedback, and cost | Delivered-commitment record and renew/change/stop decision |

**Match the ask to product readiness.** Until the four pathways pass the plan's release gates (Phase 3), the day-21–30 agreement is for role validation, rubric review, or a career-explorer session on the existing security rooms (Phase 0) — not a learner cohort. The [pilot proposal](career-pilot-proposal.md)'s cohort is scheduled only once those gates are met; say so in the first conversation rather than offering dates the product cannot meet.

Targets are acquisition hypotheses: 12 researched prospects → 6 carefully selected first approaches → 3 conversations → 1 scoped pilot. These are not a forecast or evidence of partner interest. Follow up once after about five working days and once later if appropriate; stop when declined or asked not to continue.

Before activation, record a written scope covering owners, deliverables, cohort eligibility, review capacity, costs/payment, cancellation, confidentiality, IP/content permissions, data sharing, branding, opportunity details and reporting. A short bounded pilot agreement is more useful than a broad logo-only MOU.

Track `researched → route verified → contacted → discovery → proposed → agreed → delivered → renewed/closed`. Count an active partner only when an agreed commitment is being delivered. Track introductions, reviewed cases, funded places, and actual opportunities separately.

## 4. Draft outreach messages

These are drafts to customize and send only when the founder chooses to contact the organization.

### Local community or university

Subject: Small cybersecurity career-pathway pilot with [organization]

Hello [team/role],

We are developing Areté to help beginners choose a cybersecurity direction through a short quiz, build a practical portfolio, and receive assessment and career feedback. We would like to explore a small opt-in pilot with [organization].

Could we discuss a career orientation and a capped cohort of [agreed number]? We would provide the tasks, support and assessed feedback; we are asking for an approved invitation route and one coordinator. We can share the demo, rubric, dates and delivery budget before any commitment. We would report aggregate outcomes and share individual work only with the learner's permission.

Would a short call with the person responsible for training or career development be appropriate?

[Founder / Areté contact]

### Practitioner or employer

Subject: Would you review a beginner [SOC/GRC/AppSec] portfolio task?

Hello [team/role],

We are developing a career-focused cybersecurity pathway on Areté and want the tasks to reflect what beginners can actually do under supervision. Your work in [verified relevant area] makes your feedback particularly useful.

Our initial request is a short role-validation conversation and, if appropriate, a review of [one rubric / three anonymized portfolio summaries]. We can agree time and compensation in advance. Areté would provide the case, assessment criteria and a concise feedback form.

If that proves useful, we could separately discuss a supervised project or a current opportunity for candidates who meet your requirements. We are not assuming a hiring commitment.

[Founder / Areté contact]

### Sponsor

Subject: Fund a defined cybersecurity career cohort with assessed outcomes

Hello [team/role],

We would like to propose funding [number] Areté career-pathway places for [agreed audience]. The scope includes practical work, human assessment, portfolio feedback and career support. Funding would cover an agreed itemized budget, including reviewer time and any access support.

We would report enrollment, completed assessed work, delivered career actions and follow-up outcomes with clear denominators. The proposal does not promise employment, and individual learner information would require separate permission.

May we send the one-page pilot proposal to the appropriate program team?

[Founder / Areté contact]

## 5. Initial learner groups and approved recruitment channels

Working interpretation of “list of uses”: cover both target users and the ways they use Areté, with recruitment channels. This list describes prospective audiences and use cases; it is **not** a list of recruited people, confirmed customers, or registered accounts. Do not fabricate names, scrape closed groups, or add individuals to marketing lists without their agreement.

| Prospective learner group | Career need / use case | Recruitment route to seek | Initial offer |
|---|---|---|---|
| UNIUYO cybersecurity students | Choose a direction and turn class learning into evidence | Approved departmental orientation or voluntary class-rep invitation | Career quiz, first task, pilot waitlist |
| Computer science / computing students | Discover security operations, risk or secure development work | Approved computing student community invitation | Role tasters and portfolio examples |
| SIWES-seeking students | Prepare relevant work samples and explain skills before placement | SIWES coordinator discussion; institution-approved invitation | Placement preparation clinic; no promised SIWES credit |
| Uyo technology-community beginners | Move from general interest to a realistic first pathway | Start Innovation Hub/Roothub event collaboration if agreed | Career-discovery session and opt-in cohort |
| Junior developers | Demonstrate access-control analysis and remediation | Approved developer event or partner-hosted clinic | AppSec taster and finding template |
| Helpdesk / IT support learners | Explore account investigation and escalation work | Employer-approved staff development or public IT community | SOC foundations diagnostic and case |
| Business, audit or accounting learners | Understand whether GRC work fits their interests | Approved student/professional group and ISACA discussion | GRC case and management briefing |
| Women exploring cybersecurity | Obtain accessible direction, practice and mentoring | Foundation/community collaboration if accepted | Opt-in career session and funded places if secured |
| Graduates / early-career job seekers | Identify gaps and build credible examples to discuss | Approved graduate/career event, direct opt-in landing page | Pathway plus portfolio and interview review |
| Employer-sponsored junior staff | Develop a specific bounded capability | Direct training-needs discovery with an employer | Defined cohort outcome, authorized reporting and support |

Start a small recruitment funnel: approved invitation → clear landing page → opt-in interest → quiz → actual first task → cohort place. A proposed initial source mix is five university learners, five local-community learners and five independent opt-ins; adjust to actual demand and reviewer capacity rather than claiming access to these groups.

The opt-in form should capture name, contact, location/timezone, career goal, experience, availability, access needs, source, and permission to contact. Record consent date/source and a simple withdrawal route. Employer-sharing permission is a separate later choice. Store real learner contacts in an access-controlled system, not the public repository or this prospect CSV.

If the intended request is an **existing registered-user list**, obtain it through an authorized read-only account/admin query: account ID, creation date, learning mode, and only the contact/activity fields needed for the stated purpose. Do not expose credentials or infer recruitment consent from registration. No live account database was queried during this public-source research.

Finish one demonstrable quiz-to-portfolio slice; prepare the pilot pack; reconfirm the local university, Start Innovation Hub, Roothub and professional-community contact routes; identify independent blue/SOC, red/ethical testing, GRC and AppSec reviewers; and seek employer feedback on the role tasks before claiming a hiring pipeline.
